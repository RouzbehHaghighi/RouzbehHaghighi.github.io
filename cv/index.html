Professional Summary

Doctoral researcher at the intersection of power-system reliability and artificial intelligence, working in two complementary directions — AI for the grid (large language models and reinforcement learning as operational decision-makers) and the grid for AI (integrating large-scale, uncertain AI data-center loads into distribution and transmission networks).

Experience

  • 2023 - present

    Dearborn, MI, USA

    Graduate Student Research Assistant
    University of Michigan-Dearborn, Dept. of ECE
    Doctoral research on power-system reliability and AI — AI for the grid and the grid for AI.
    • Grid integration of AI data-center loads: hosting-capacity and reliability frameworks quantifying the impact of large, uncertain AI data-center demand on distribution networks, with grid-aware load-coordination and energy-storage strategies.
    • LLMs as power-system decision-makers: fine-tuned and multi-agent LLMs for surrogate scheduling, economic dispatch, decentralized Volt/VAR control, and reliability-oriented clustering of DER-rich active distribution networks.
    • Reliability assessment under high IBR penetration: stochastic, probabilistic frameworks for reliability and resource-adequacy metrics in grids dominated by inverter-based resources.
    • Strategic and economic modeling of storage and carbon policy: Stackelberg–Bayesian and deep-reinforcement-learning models for second-life-battery investment and EV-charging optimization under AI-driven load growth.
    • Contributor to NSF project “Enhancing Grid Reliability and Stability with Distributed Energy Resources” (Award #2321661).
